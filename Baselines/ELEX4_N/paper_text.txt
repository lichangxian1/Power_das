Two energy eﬃcient unsigned approximate multipliers with N-4 compres-
sors
Xiaohui Zhang1, 2, Yi Zhan1, a), Zenghui Yu1, Yinglei Dong1, 2, and Yadong Wang1, 2
Abstract
Approximate computing stands as a highly promising approach
in curbing power consumption and enhancing energy eﬃciency in error-
tolerant applications, such as image processing and neural networks. This
brief introduces two unsigned approximate multiplier designs: MUL1, tai-
lored for high-precision applications, and MUL2, optimized for low-power
usage. Furthermore, we propose Approximate N-4 compressors, which
are devised based on input reordering circuits, along with a novel approx-
imate 4-2 compressor that align positive and negative approximations for
input patterns sharing the same probability. These proposed approximate
multipliers have been synthesized using 40nm technology, demonstrating
remarkable improvements. In comparison to the exact multiplier, the ﬁrst
proposed multiplier boasts an 84.98% enhancement in the power-delay-
area product (PDAP), while the second multiplier achieves an even more
signiﬁcant improvement of 97.22% in this parameter.
Keywords: approximate computing, multiplier, N-4 compressors, high pre-
cision design, low-power design, image processing
Classiﬁcation:
Integrated circuits
1.
Introduction
Constrained by human perception, a discrepancy exists be-
tween the precision demanded by humans and the accuracy
delivered by exact computing systems. Tailoring the calcu-
lation accuracy to align with human requirements can signif-
icantly enhance energy eﬃciency, and approximate comput-
ing serves as an eﬃcient method to modulate this accuracy
[1, 2].
Approximate computing holds promise in various error-
tolerant applications, including image processing [3, 6, 18,
29] and neural networks [2, 4, 19]. Multipliers, as fundamen-
tal subsystems in digital signal processors, ﬁnd applications
across a broad spectrum, from ﬁltering to neural networks.
Nevertheless, multipliers possess intricate designs and of-
ten contribute signiﬁcantly to power consumption and delay
time in system designs. Consequently, approximate multi-
pliers have garnered signiﬁcant attention in recent years, as
they oﬀer a potential solution to these challenges.
Approximate unsigned multipliers are widely utilized in
numerous approximation applications. The computational
process of an unsigned multiplier typically encompasses
three stages: partial product generation, partial product com-

Esposito et al. [3] initially introduced an algorithm that
redistributed the probabilities of 0 and 1 in a partial product,
validating its eﬀectiveness across various operand lengths.
Ahmadinejad et al. [4] presented novel 5-2 and 4-1 compres-
sors, along with three approximate multipliers leveraging
these compressors. They employed NAND gates for gen-
erating partial products and a highly eﬃcient compressor
to enhance the PDAP. Yi et al. [5] proposed a 4-2 com-
pressor with a complementary error strategy, optimizing the
circuit size at the transistor level to operate eﬀectively at
low supply voltages. In [6], one majority gate was used to
an ultra-eﬃcient imprecise 4:2 compressor which the sum
output is equal to ‘1’. Signiﬁcantly simpliﬁes the structure
and number of transistors compared to precise 4-2 compres-
sors. In literature [7], three approximate 4-2 compressors
were introduced and subsequently combined to create two
low-cost multipliers. This approach took into account the
dependency between neighboring columns, utilizing distinct
compressors for adjacent partial products. Duan et al. [8]
introduced an N:2 compressor with uniform output weight,
oﬀering improved resource utilization and reduced latency.
Shankar et al. [9] developed a truncation-based approximate
multiplier that leverages diverse compressors, including 4-2,
5-2, and 6-3 conﬁgurations. Without compromising the crit-
ical path, carry circuit was applied to compensate and reduce
the error. Sabetzadeh et al. [10] truncated half of product to a
constant, the other half was calculated precisely and eﬀective
error compensation module (ECM) was applied to improve
the accuracy. Static segmentation has been investigated in
approximate multipliers [11, 28], where the continuous m
bits were extracted from the two n-bit operands and trans-
ferred to the m-bit multiplier (m < n), and the ﬁnal output
was obtained after correction and suitable shift. Compared
to most of the diﬀerent weight compressions (carry, sum),
Joshi et al. [12] applied the carry prediction and truncation
techniques to the approximate multiplier, which resulted in
a signiﬁcant reduction in power delay and area, while hav-
ing no signiﬁcant eﬀect on the normalized error distance.
Priya et al. [13] presented a low-power, low-area approxi-
mate multiplier featuring an error compensation function.
The proposed multiplier considers the least signiﬁcant bit in
the product as a ﬁxed compensation term, and the remain-
ing diﬀerences are calculated through the approximation and
the exact part, so as to realize the trade-oﬀbetween accu-
racy and eﬃciency. Approximate logarithmic multipliers

In general, the approximate multiplier is vertically seg-
mented into three key regions: the precision region, the
approximation region, and the truncation region [17]. The
proposed multiplier adheres to this three-part categoriza-
tion. The principle contributions of this brief are outlined
as follows:
1) During the partial product generation stage, we opt for
the NAND-gate instead of the conventional AND-gate. The
resulting output is then fed directly into the compressor. No-
tably, the NAND-gate exhibits superior performance com-
pared to the AND-gate [10].
2) In the partial product compression stage, a novel 4-2 com-
pressor with align positive and negative approximations is
applied to reduce error accumulation and enhance electrical
performance.
3) In the truncation part, we adopt a constant approxima-
tion method, utilizing a speciﬁc constant to further reﬁne
accuracy.
This brief is structured as follows: Section 2 introduces
the compressors relevant to the proposed designs and their
features. Section 3 shows the proposed architecture of mul-
tiplier. In Sect. 4, we present the simulation results and the
comparison with previous approximate multipliers. Finally,
Sect. 5 concludes this brief.
2.
Compressor
2.1
Exact compressor
Compressors are most commonly used for implementing the
partial products reduction stage. Full-adder is most basic
exact 3-2 compressor and an exact 4-2 compressor is im-
plemented with two full-adders. Exact 4-2 compressor has
ﬁve inputs and three outputs so it should more be called 5-3
compressor. Higher order exact 5-2 compressors is achieved
by three full adders which should be called 7-4 compressor.
The schematics of compressor are illustrated in Fig. 1. For
special structural designs, exact compressors do not have
long carry chains, however, the large number of transistors
brings a non-negligible power consumption.

As the importance of the energy consumption rising in in-
tegrated circuits, the power consumption from exact com-
pressor circuits has been paid more attention. Approximate
compressors [20–25, 30] are getting more attention.

The structure of the proposed unsigned approximate 8-bit
multiplier design MUL1 is illustrated in Fig. 5 which more
focus on high accuracy between the hardware and accuracy
characteristics. For trade-oﬀaccuracy and power, the partial
product is divided into three regions in columns, exact, ap-
proximate and truncation. The four least signiﬁcant columns
of the partial products are truncated with constants ‘1000’.
Columns four to ten are regarded as the approximate region,
where the approximate N-4 compressors are used in stage
one. The input data of approximate compressors is obtained
through the NAND gate, which is more advantageous in area
and power compared to the AND gate. The four most sig-
niﬁcant columns and partial products compression in stage
2 are calculated precisely. For the ﬁnal addition, a 10-bit
ripple carry adder (RCA) is employed.

4.
Experimental results
4.1
Accuracy analysis
The accuracy factors of the proposed multipliers are inves-
tigated in this section based on the results obtained by the
MODELSIM simulations. For evaluating the output quality
of the proposed unsigned approximate multipliers, error rate
(ER), normalized mean error distance (NMED) and mean
relative error distance (MRED) is considered.
ER represents the proportion of the number of error re-
sults in the total output. MRED describes the mean value
of the relative error distance. In order to facilitate the com-
parison of approximate multipliers with diﬀerent bit widths,
NMED normalizes the average error distance to measure the
deviation of the calculated result from the exact value.
To obtain reliable error metrics, we go through all 65536
input combinations.
The accuracy metrics results of the
multipliers are presented in Table III. The results indicate
high error rate for our designs due to the truncation of the
least signiﬁcant columns.
NMED and MRED of MUL1
achieve highest levels than the other counterparts due to its
structure and the eﬃcient compressor module. Meanwhile,
MUL2 has the worst parameters due to the larger truncation.
4.2
Hardware analysis
All mentioned approximate multipliers are modeled using
Verilog HDL, and synthesized by Synopsys Design Com-
piler using a commercial SMIC 40 nm library under supply
voltage of 1.1 V. Perform functional simulation for 65536
input combinations and generate saif ﬁle of signal ﬂip prob-
ability. Since the designed approximate multipliers are all
pure combinatory logic, a virtual clock with a frequency of
100 Mhz is set in the constraint ﬁle. Set INV cell of library as
input drive cell and 40 times the input capacity of AND2/A
pin as output load. The power-delay-area product (PDAP) is
regard as the normalize metrics of trade-oﬀ. The evaluation
results are shown in Table IV.
The results indicate that the proposed multipliers have su-
perior delay, power, power-delay-area product (PDAP) and
area compared to the existing multipliers.
The proposed
approximate multiplier MUL2 has unquestionably the best
PDAP reduction. Compared with the exact multiplier, the
delay, area, power consumption and PDAP are reduced by
45.49%, 77.17%, 77.62% and 97.22% respectively but suf-
fers from the largest NMED and MRED. MUL1 has worse
latency, area, and power consumption compared to compa-
rable designs, but has the highest NMED and MRED, PDAP
down by 84.98% compared to the exact multiplier.

4.3
Image multiplication
Image multiplication is the multiplication of two gray-scale
images with corresponding pixels, which only contains mul-
tiplication, so it can directly reﬂect the eﬀectiveness of
the approximate multiplier.
The peak signal-to-noise ra-
tio (PSNR) value and structural similarity (SSIM) value of
the approximate image are obtained by using MATLAB tool
to evaluate the quality of the approximate image, and the
average value of all photos is used as the ﬁnal index, so as to
verify the eﬀectiveness of the approximate multiplier in the
application of image multiplication.
The PSNR and SSIM values of diﬀerent approximate im-
ages are listed in Table V, which are used to quantify the
quality of approximate images. The higher the accuracy of
the approximate multiplier, the higher the PSNR and SSIM
values of the output image. When the value of PSNR is
greater than 30 dB, it indicates that the image quality is ex-
cellent. MUL1 oﬀers the average PSNR and SSIM values
of more than 58 dB and 0.9993, respectively, which are best
image quality. MUL2 oﬀers the average PSNR and SSIM
values of more than 43 dB and 0.9781, respectively, which
are more than enough for image processing applications.
Figure 7 shows three resulting graphs from exact multipli-
cation, MUL1, and MUL2. From the subjective view of the
naked eye, it is diﬃcult to ﬁnd the diﬀerence between the
output images of diﬀerent approximate multipliers.
